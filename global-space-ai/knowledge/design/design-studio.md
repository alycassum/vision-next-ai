## How a design project runs
1. Site measurement: the team measures the plot or space physically to get exact dimensions and work out how much can be built under the area's bylaws.
2. As-built drawing: a 2D CAD layout of the plot with the permitted construction calculated.
3. Discovery meeting: the client shares the style of house or space they like (elevation pictures), and the conversation covers interiors and furniture taste, so the drawings reflect their preferences.
4. 2D layouts: full floor plans (ground floor, upper floors, and basement if applicable) with total covered area, drafted in the format the approving authority accepts.
5. 3D mockups: rendered visuals so the client can see the look and feel before anything is built.
6. Progress updates: the client is kept informed as drawings progress.
7. Approval process: drawings are verified and stamped by an architect approved by the authority, then submitted in the required number of copies. Expect a few rounds of revisions and resubmission (often three to four) depending on the authority's checks and the latest rules.
8. Approval: the approval package is issued by the authority.

## Notes
- Approval timelines depend on the authority and cannot be promised. The team gives an estimate after reviewing the plot.
- Design can be done for houses, offices, restaurants, retail and hospitality spaces.
- Clients can bring their own preferences, references and furniture ideas.
